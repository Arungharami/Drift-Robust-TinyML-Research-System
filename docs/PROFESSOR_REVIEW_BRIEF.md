# Professor Review Brief

This project studies drift-robust, explainable TinyML for electronic-nose sensing. The practical problem is that gas-sensor arrays change over time through aging, contamination, and environmental variation. A model that appears accurate under a random split may have trained on samples whose characteristics resemble the future test period. The primary evaluation is therefore chronological: `FIXED_ORIGIN` provides the prospective deployment-oriented estimate, `EXPANDING_WINDOW` studies adaptation, and `IID_DIAGNOSTIC` remains diagnostic rather than the headline result.

The scientific design uses training-only preprocessing and four frozen model families. The evidence portal derives statuses and artifacts from the repository registry, rather than presenting a progress percentage. That distinction matters: execution, validation, protocol freezing, failure, and blocking are all retained rather than collapsed into a single success label.

The current XAI record requires interpretation. Stage 10 explanation-fidelity and Stage 11 chronological-stability experiments were executed, but the candidate claims tested in those stages were unsupported. This is not an erased result. It may indicate weak explanation behavior under the chosen tests, limitations in the definitions, or a need for sensitivity analysis. Stage 12 measured resource-aware XAI cost under its recorded environment. Stage 13 froze the embedded protocol. Stages 14 and 14R retain failed FP32 export/preprocessing outcomes; subsequent host-side equivalence work does not establish physical deployment.

Physical verification is the immediate operational constraint. The Stage 15 prerequisite detection was executed, but no supported nRF52840 board or debug interface was detected. Board identity was not guessed. Consequently, deployment, Flash and SRAM usage, MCU latency, and PPK2 energy are `NOT_MEASURED`; the hardware gate is `BLOCKED_HARDWARE`, not complete.

The provisional contribution is systems-level: a reproducible chain from chronological drift evaluation through explanation fidelity/stability and computational cost to numerical equivalence and physical deployment cost. It is not necessarily a new learning algorithm. For advisor discussion, three decisions are central: whether the evaluation hierarchy and model set are appropriate; how to interpret the unsupported explanation claims; and what evidence bar is required for publication, including hardware measurements, another e-nose dataset, venue positioning, and the sufficiency of a systems contribution. This brief does not change metrics, experiments, evidence JSON, or deployment state.
